BPYG-172 Philosophy of Religion: how to study for the assignment

A study map for BPYG-172 Philosophy of Religion: the three official blocks, turning a question into an argument plan, writing in your own words and avoiding common mistakes.

Start with your own assignment paper

Before you read anything else, open the official Current Assignments page and search for BPYG-172. Match your programme, medium and the session the assignment is valid for, then download the paper. Read the questions, any choice pattern, the word limits and the submission instructions in that PDF. Confirm the deadline and how to submit from the official notice that applies to you.

A similar course name or a store label is not proof that a paper is the right one. Compare the exact code with your own enrolment record. Then write a checklist of every part of every question, and note which unit each part seems to belong to before you start writing.

Where to start with Philosophy of Religion

BPYG-172 is Philosophy of Religion. The course asks you to think about religious ideas and arguments in a careful, academic way. It does not ask you to defend or attack any faith. Your task in an answer is to explain positions accurately, show the reasons behind them, and examine them fairly. Keep your tone respectful and your claims tied to your course material.

Begin with the question. Write in one line what it asks you to do, then list the parts. Next to each part, note the block and unit where your course text deals with it. Only then start reading in detail, with your notes organised by question.

The three blocks as a reading map

The official eGyanKosh page for BPYG-172 lists three blocks: Introduction to the Philosophy of Religion; Problem of Affirming God's Existence; and Religious Pluralism. This is a reading map, not a list of expected questions. Match each real question in your paper to a block and unit. If a question connects two blocks, check the notes of both, but keep only the material that supports the focus of the question.

In the introduction block, note how the course defines its subject and its key terms. In the existence block, keep the arguments and the objections your unit gives apart, and record how each argument is built. In the pluralism block, note the different positions the unit describes and what each says about the relationship between religions. Write about a position only after reading how your unit presents it.

Turn the question into an argument plan

For each part of the question, write a plan with four lines: the claim you will make or explain, the reasons from your unit, the objection or alternative the unit mentions, and the connection back to the question. If you cannot fill a line, that shows exactly what to read again.

Compare questions need shared dimensions, explain questions need clear steps, and critical questions need a fair statement of a view before the objections. Do not use the answer to argue for your own belief unless the question asks for your reasoned position, and then give reasons.

Write in your own words without changing the meaning

Philosophical ideas are easy to distort when you paraphrase. After reading a passage, note the main point, the reason and the limit. Write it in your own sentences, then compare with the text. Check that you have not made a position stronger or weaker than the text does. Keep technical terms and explain them in simple sentences. If you quote, keep it short, use quotation marks and give the source.

If your assignment is allowed in a particular language or medium, follow the paper's instruction. Whatever you choose, use the course terms consistently, and make sure the whole answer reads clearly in simple, complete sentences.

A practice outline for a comparison (hypothetical)

For practice, imagine a question that asks you to compare two ways of thinking about the relationship between different religions. This is not from any assignment. Outline it like this: the shared question; position A and its main reasons as your unit presents them; position B in the same way; two dimensions on which they differ; one fair limit of each; and a closing line about what the comparison shows.

Fill in the boxes only from your units. If a box stays empty, read again. Do not fill it from memory or from an unchecked summary. Your real answer must be your own work and follow the instructions in your official paper.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

  • Writing a general essay on belief instead of answering the question. Fix: check each paragraph against a part of the question.
  • Presenting a position in a weak form. Fix: write the version its supporters would accept.
  • Mixing arguments from different blocks without saying so. Fix: label which argument you are discussing.
  • Unsupported claims about what "all religions" believe. Fix: say which position or which unit you mean.
  • Long quotations in place of explanation. Fix: explain in your own sentences.

A short study routine, from reading to final review

Map the questions to the blocks. Read the units with the question plan beside you. Write one page of notes per question. Draft from the notes with the book closed. Review the draft against the question and your plan, then do a final pass for accuracy of terms and for clear, respectful wording.

If you notice a gap, fix it by reading your unit and, if needed, asking your academic support. Any private study material is optional help and can contain errors, so check it against your course text.

Can I use a personal religious example?

Only if it fits the question and you keep it respectful and clearly marked as an example. It cannot replace the arguments from your course material. Do not make claims about what a whole community believes unless your unit does so.

Is it fine to submit the assignment in Hinglish?

Follow the medium and language rules in your official assignment paper. If the paper allows only certain languages, use one of them. Whatever language you use, keep your terms consistent and your sentences clear.

Will these topics definitely come in my assignment?

No. The block titles are only for organising your study. Check the real questions, instructions and validity in the official assignment PDF. This page does not predict questions and does not guarantee marks or acceptance.
