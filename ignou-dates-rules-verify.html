How to verify IGNOU dates and rules

Forwarded screenshots are not proof. Learn how to trace an IGNOU notice to its source, check scope and session, and keep a simple verification note.

From a forwarded screenshot to the original notice

Admission, assignment, exam form and result updates spread fast in student groups. A forwarded message can be a useful lead, but being forwarded many times does not make it accurate. A screenshot that says "urgent" or "official" is not enough either. You need the original notice, the office that issued it, and how it applies to your programme.

This guide describes a process for checking. It does not announce any current deadline, fee or rule. Before you act on anything, read the current official instructions that apply to you.

Make your question exact. Instead of "What is the IGNOU date?", ask: "What are the assignment submission instructions for my programme and session?" Admission, re-registration, exam form and re-evaluation are different processes, so an extension for one does not apply to another. Put your programme code, session, mode and the course at the top of your note.

Make the official website your starting point

Start from the main IGNOU website and follow its links to student services, notices and portals. Do not land on a payment page from a search result title. Read the web address carefully. A page does not become official because it names the university. A padlock icon does not prove who runs a site, and you should know where a link in an unknown message really goes before you open it.

For assignments, the official Current Assignments page lets you search by programme or course code. When we read it, it listed the medium, the session each assignment is valid for, maximum marks, weightage and the due dates for each term-end. The page also tells you to check the validity before downloading, so open the file and compare its details. Do not assume the right session from a file name or a cover image.

If you cannot find the paper you need, a look-alike PDF from a private seller is not an official replacement. Ask your programme or regional support. Official questions and private reference material are different things.

Read the whole notice, not the headline

In the original, check the subject, issue date, process, which programmes it covers, the session and the conditions. A notice can be for one region, mode, student group or exam category, and a forwarded summary may make it sound wider. A photo of the headline is not enough. Read the full document and the instructions it links to, including any attachment. An important exception can sit in the last paragraph.

The issue date and the action deadline are different fields. "Released on" is not "last date". Always write the year with a date. If a screenshot has no year, do not guess the current one. If you see "extended", check which process and which group it applies to. An old blog date turned into a fresh screenshot does not make a notice current.

A simple verification note

Keep a note on your phone with these fields: process, programme, session, source link, notice date, applicable condition, action deadline and checked-on date. Write "unconfirmed" in any field you could not confirm. Save the PDF if you like, but also save the link to the original page. If a new notice appears, note how it relates to the old one. A screenshot folder alone does not show what applies to you.

Two illustrations, not real notices. A group posts "assignment date extended", but the original notice is for a different session. Your note shows the mismatch, so you do not change your calendar. In another case, the date in the message is for form submission, not the start of the exam. The "process" field keeps you from mixing them up.

What to ask your regional or study centre

University-wide notices may sit alongside regional or study-centre arrangements. Check which part of the process is on the central portal and which needs local instructions. Tell your centre your programme, course and session. "Where do I submit my assignment?" works better with that context. Another city's experience does not confirm your centre's process.

Get contact details from the main IGNOU website or an official centre page. Never send an ID, password or money to an unverified group number. Save written replies together with the relevant notice. If you got an answer by phone, ask for a link to the written instruction. Do not assume a response time. Keep your question short: the process, your context, the conflicting sources and exactly what you need clarified.

When two sources disagree

Compare them on programme, session, mode and process. One source may be for an older exam cycle and another for a specific group. A newer date is a useful clue, but a newer notice does not always replace every older rule, so read any wording about extension, correction or superseding. If the form on screen and the notice disagree, save both as evidence and ask official support. Do not pick one date for yourself while it is unclear which applies. Hiding the uncertainty does not make your plan safer.

Blogs, coaching guides and student messages can explain, but they cannot override an official instruction. If you cannot find the source, say it is not verified. When you forward a conflicting screenshot, include the original link and say what is uncertain.

Sharing and organising updates

When you share an update, send the official link, the notice date and its limited scope, and keep your own interpretation separate from the original wording. "This notice says X for this session" is safer than "everyone's date is extended". Do not circulate an old article as a current announcement, and if you get a correction, correct your earlier message too.

Keep notes or folders for assignments, exams, results and follow-ups. Look at the original source again before an important action, because a saved screenshot is not proof of the current state. A reminder tells you when to check. It does not verify a notice. The aim is not to browse dozens of sites a day. It is to match the right action to the right source. Your checklist: the official original page, the right programme, the right session, the full instructions, the latest update and saved evidence.

Is an official-looking logo enough?

No. A logo can appear in a screenshot or on a private page. Check the page address, whether it is linked from the main IGNOU website, who issued the notice and who it applies to. If you cannot recover the source, treat the screenshot as an unverified lead.

What if an old article ranks first in search?

Search ranking is not proof that something is current. Check the dates, the year and the session of both the article and the notice it links to. An explanation can stay useful, but do not reuse an old deadline. Confirm the action on the current official pages before you set a reminder or pay.

What if the official page does not open?

Do not fill the gap with a guess. Look for programme or regional support, and note the error and the process you need. Treat a cached PDF as history, not as current confirmation. If the decision is time-sensitive, tell official support exactly which point is unresolved.

Official sources

The IGNOU home page and Current Assignments page were read on 1 October 2026. The Current Assignments page lists the programme, course, medium, valid session and due dates. Dates change, so use the live pages.
